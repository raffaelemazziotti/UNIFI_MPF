CDKL5 Deficiency Disorder Monitoring Biomarker Teaching Dataset

Scientific & Clinical Context

This synthetic dataset represents a human longitudinal study in children with CDKL5 deficiency disorder. The goal is to explore whether baseline physiological and psychophysical measurements can help predict broad future prognosis status over follow-up. The variables are intentionally noisy and overlapping to reflect an early monitoring biomarker setting.

Experimental Paradigm

Participants completed resting EEG, pupillometry during a simple visual task, galvanic skin response recording during repeated sensory stimulation, and brief psychophysical procedures adapted for developmental ability. Caregivers also completed short ratings of arousal regulation and daily sensory behavior. Follow-up visits were used to assign a two-level prognosis category based on overall clinical course.

Data Modalities & Variables

  • Age_Years — age at baseline in years
  • EEG_ThetaPower_uV2 — resting theta-band power in microvolt-squared units
  • EEG_PeakFrequency_Hz — dominant resting EEG peak frequency in hertz
  • Pupil_DilationPct — average task-evoked pupil dilation as percent change from baseline
  • GSR_Response_uS — mean galvanic skin response amplitude in microsiemens during stimulation blocks
  • Visual_Detection_Threshold_pct — minimum visual contrast detected reliably in percent
  • Tactile_Threshold_mN — tactile detection threshold in millinewtons
  • ReactionTime_ms — mean response time on the psychophysical task in milliseconds
  • Arousal_Regulation_Score — caregiver-rated arousal regulation score on a 0–100 scale
  • Sensory_Behavior_Score — caregiver-rated sensory behavior score on a 0–100 scale
  • Baseline_Clinical_State — baseline clinical state category (Stable-range or More-affected-range)
  • Future_Prognosis — follow-up prognosis category (Stable or Worsened)

Groups

All participants in this dataset have CDKL5 deficiency disorder and were assessed at baseline and follow-up. The prognosis categories summarize later clinical course and are intended as the prediction target rather than a grouping variable.

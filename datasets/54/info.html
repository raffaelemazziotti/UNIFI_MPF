Synthetic teaching dataset

Social Anxiety Disorder Monitoring Biomarker Teaching Dataset

Scientific & Clinical Context

This synthetic dataset represents a human longitudinal study in adolescents with social anxiety disorder. The study was built for teaching early monitoring biomarker work where baseline physiological and psychophysical variables have very little ability to explain future clinical state. Most columns are intentionally noisy and only weakly structured.

Experimental Paradigm

Participants completed resting EEG, pupillometry during a face-viewing task, galvanic skin response recording during brief social-evaluative cues, and simple psychophysical visual threshold and temporal discrimination procedures. They also completed short self-report ratings of somatic tension and anticipatory discomfort. Follow-up visits were used to assign a two-level future clinical state.

Data Modalities & Variables

  • Age_Years — age at baseline in years
  • EEG_AlphaPower_uV2 — resting alpha-band power in microvolt-squared units
  • EEG_N1Latency_ms — visual evoked N1 latency in milliseconds
  • Pupil_DilationPct — average task-evoked pupil dilation as percent change from baseline
  • GSR_Response_uS — mean galvanic skin response amplitude in microsiemens during cue blocks
  • Visual_Threshold_pct — minimum visual contrast detected reliably in percent
  • Temporal_Discrimination_ms — temporal discrimination threshold in milliseconds
  • ReactionTime_ms — mean response time on the psychophysical task in milliseconds
  • Somatic_Tension_Score — self-reported somatic tension score on a 0–100 scale
  • Anticipatory_Discomfort_Score — self-reported anticipatory discomfort score on a 0–100 scale
  • Baseline_Clinical_State — baseline clinical state category (Lower-support or Higher-support)
  • Future_Clinical_State — follow-up clinical state category (Lower-support or Higher-support)

Groups

All participants in this dataset have social anxiety disorder and were assessed at baseline and follow-up. The future clinical state is the intended target for monitoring biomarker exploration.
